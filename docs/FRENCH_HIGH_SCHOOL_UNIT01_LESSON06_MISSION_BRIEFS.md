# Khaemenes Academy · French I–IV · Unit 01 Lesson 06 mission briefs

**Status:** Instructional design proposals, not released course pages. The source assessments and score engine are explicitly non-official. These briefs add performance evidence but do not replace the original course lessons.

## Common student experience

Each mission uses an approachable sequence: **Explore → Practice → Create → Check → Reflect**. The teacher/parent may offer directions in English while encouraging increasing French production. Learners may repeat practice without losing previously recorded work. A failed submitted assessment should open a correction activity before a separately logged new draw. Listening and oral performance remain human-reviewed evidence, **not automatically inferred from multiple-choice results**.

Common required artifacts: one interpretive response with a cited or provided source, one original written product, one interpersonal performance (live exchange or supported recording), an error-correction note, and a short reflection. No student-identifying information is needed in prompts. Provide captions, transcripts, keyboard-first paths, reduced motion, and printable alternatives.

### French I · The Welcome Desk & Café

**Challenge:** Help a visiting French-speaking classmate navigate their first school morning and order a snack.

- **Explore:** Interpret a short welcome card, timetable and bilingual café menu. Identify names, introductions, two times, ingredient concerns and prices; provide text transcripts for any dialogue.
- **Practice:** Spell your own fictional name with the right diacritics; choose polite or familiar greetings; describe two classmates using gender/number agreement; distinguish `du`, `de la`, `de l'`, `des` and the negative `de` with feedback.
- **Create:** Write a six–eight line welcome dialogue with a greeting, name question, classroom request, food order, price and departure. Add a labeled imaginary timetable.
- **Communicate:** Role-play a 2–3 minute exchange. A partner asks one unanticipated follow-up about a price, ingredient or classroom location. Students may prepare a vocabulary aid but should not read all responses.
- **Reflect:** Correct two authentic mistakes and explain an accent or article choice.

**Review targets:** Comprehensible beginner speech, correct introductions, practical politeness, readable simple sentences, and evidence of understanding food/allergy questions. Do not claim a student can handle real food-allergy safety based solely on a quiz.

### French II · The Missing Travel Notebook

**Challenge:** Reconstruct a journey from a fictional traveler's ticket, diary fragments and messages without inventing undocumented facts.

- **Explore:** Read three dated diary snippets and a simple ticket; separate continuing background, repeated habits and completed events.
- **Practice:** Sort passé composé / imparfait; compare `avoir` and `être` auxiliaries and subject agreement; repair direct/indirect object pronoun placement; explain a past participle agreeing with a preceding direct object.
- **Create:** Produce a 140–180-word narrative: a habitual situation, an unexpected event, a three-event timeline and a sourced uncertainty. Include at least one legitimate `y` or `en` substitution.
- **Communicate:** Tell the story for 3–4 minutes; answer two questions about chronology and identify one gap in the evidence.
- **Reflect:** Annotate corrections in tense choice, pronoun reference and agreement.

**Review targets:** Past temporal structure, accurate auxiliary choice, transparent narrative evidence and clear interpersonal responses. Grade grammar separately from creativity and source integrity.

### French III · The Community Proposal Forum

**Challenge:** Recommend a modest community initiative from two fictional, intentionally limited reports without overstating their conclusions.

- **Explore:** Compare a neighborhood survey with a small-budget summary. Record each source's population, year, method and limitation; identify one metric the sources cannot directly compare.
- **Practice:** Distinguish future commitments from conditional proposals; use `si` + imperfect → present conditional correctly; construct `qui`, `que`, `où` and `dont` clauses; practice respectful counterarguments.
- **Create:** Write a 220–280-word evidence-based proposal with a qualified claim, two citations to provided reports, one fair counterargument, a conditional pilot and a measurable success criterion.
- **Communicate:** Lead a 4–5 minute discussion with an opposing view; ask and answer evidence questions and adjust one claim in response.
- **Reflect:** Identify one correlation/causation risk and revise an over-generalized sentence.

**Review targets:** Evidence handling, responsible register, accurate advanced clauses and credible policy reasoning. Do not substitute unsupported precise statistics or claim a trial actually occurred.

### French IV · The Many Voices Exhibit

**Challenge:** Curate a small exhibit about a fictional historical relocation using an archive excerpt, staged documentary segment and literary passage.

- **Explore:** Label each provided text's genre, authorship, creation date, intended audience, translation/editorial status and evidentiary limits.
- **Practice:** Distinguish past counterfactual from documented fact; annotate `aurait` in reported vs hypothetical uses; identify voice, `nous` referents, register and unmarked translation decisions.
- **Create:** Write a 300–400-word bilingual curatorial interpretation comparing at least three source types. Include an original-language note, one carefully bounded counterfactual and a transparent citation for each excerpt.
- **Communicate:** Present a 5–6 minute exhibit walk-through and answer two questions about disagreements, omissions and the ethics of representation.
- **Reflect:** Revise one overconfident claim, disclose one editing/translation choice and identify a missing perspective without fabricating testimony.

**Review targets:** French interpretive precision, synthesis, source provenance, ethical representation and a defensible interpretation. Fictional sources must be labeled fictional; students must not fabricate historical quotations.

## Shared human-review rubric (not auto-completed)

| Criterion | Weight | Reviewer evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Interpretive accuracy and source limits | 25% | Reading/listening source response |
| Language control appropriate to grade | 25% | Original writing and oral output |
| Communicative task achievement | 20% | Dialogue/debate/presentation |
| Reasoning, genre and intercultural care | 20% | Work samples and source discussion |
| Revision and reflection | 10% | Marked corrections and reflection |

**Mastery:** Proposed ≥80% only after reviewed evidence is present; these rubrics are *not wired to formal grading*. A teacher/qualified reviewer must verify speaking, listening and source interpretation. For any inaccessible auditory source, provide a transcript but do not record listening proficiency without listening evidence.

## Unit assessment release blockers

- All four 6×27 draft question banks must pass the automated source-contract checks and an independent French-educator review.
- The **50-question unit exam** and **67-item minimum bank** are format requirements; the total 162 questions per grade is a pool, *not* a balanced or audited 67-item unit exam. A course/unit scope alone does not ensure equitable coverage.
- A balanced blueprint must define coverage across six lessons and domains, difficulty, multiple correct interpretations, question edits and exposure rules before release.
- Real evidence must remain separate from manual Hall percentages and historical learner completion. No new automatic formal grades, unlocks or certificates.
- Cross-device/accessibility tests, persistent learner-scoped attempts and printable/exportable feedback must pass before a student-facing deployment.

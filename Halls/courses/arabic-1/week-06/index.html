Arabic I — Week 06

Names and Personal Information

Goals: introduce a fictional character, ask a name, express where someone is from, and distinguish polite or grammatical address forms.

1. Vocabulary and meaningful forms

Modern Standard Arabic uses grammatical gender in some second-person forms, such as أَنْتَ and أَنْتِ. These are language forms, not assumptions about a student's identity. Respect requested forms of address and allow invented characters.

ExpressionApproximate pronunciationMeaning or use
اِسْمِي لَيْلَىismī LaylāMy name is Layla.
مَا اسْمُكَ؟mā ismuka?What is your name? (addressing a male)
مَا اسْمُكِ؟mā ismuki?What is your name? (addressing a female)
أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَanā min MiṣrI am from Egypt.
مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟min ayna anta?Where are you from? (addressing a male)
مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتِ؟min ayna anti?Where are you from? (addressing a female)
أَنَا طَالِبٌanā ṭālibunI am a student (masculine).
أَنَا طَالِبَةٌanā ṭālibatunI am a student (feminine).

Pronunciation approximations are teaching aids, not substitutions for qualified audio models.

2. Worked dialogue

أ: مَرْحَبًا! مَا اسْمُكِ؟ — Hello! What is your name? (to a female)

ب: اِسْمِي لَيْلَى. — My name is Layla.

أ: مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتِ؟ — Where are you from? (to a female)

ب: أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَ. — I am from Egypt.

Evidence question: who shares a name and where they are from? Circle the question phrases and underline the answers.

Variation: replace the fictional name and origin with a different invented character. Preserve register or grammatical agreement where relevant.

3. Four-skill practice

  1. Listen: compare the questions and answers after a qualified language model. If none is available, mark listening as pending.
  2. Speak: practice the dialogue with a partner using fictional identities; switch roles and select an appropriate address form.
  3. Read: cover the meanings and identify the written questions, names and origin statements.
  4. Write: produce a three-line introduction of an invented character in the target script. Copy accurately before attempting independent recall.

Privacy rule: personal identity, legal name, address, or country of origin must never be required in a public learner exercise.

4. Week 06 formative quiz

Ten open-book questions. Eight correct responses meet the preliminary readiness threshold; separately assess listening, speaking and writing.

5. Teacher guidance

Pacing: five lessons of 30–45 minutes. Evidence: vocabulary recognition, paired dialogue, three-line introduction, and quiz. Accessibility: give large-print text, typing alternatives, non-speaking participation alternatives and unlimited formative retries.

Misconceptions: context changes formality; grammatical gender and role words do not justify assumptions about a learner. Teacher-verified pronunciations remain essential. No learner records are stored or transmitted by this page.

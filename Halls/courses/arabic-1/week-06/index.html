Arabic I — Week 06

Names and Personal Information

Goals: introduce a fictional character, ask a name, express where someone is from, and distinguish polite or grammatical address forms.

1. Vocabulary and meaningful forms

Modern Standard Arabic uses grammatical gender in some second-person forms, such as أَنْتَ and أَنْتِ. These are language forms, not assumptions about a student's identity. Respect requested forms of address and allow invented characters.

ExpressionApproximate pronunciationMeaning or use
اِسْمِي لَيْلَىismī LaylāMy name is Layla.
مَا اسْمُكَ؟mā ismuka?What is your name? (addressing a male)
مَا اسْمُكِ؟mā ismuki?What is your name? (addressing a female)
أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَanā min MiṣrI am from Egypt.
مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتَ؟min ayna anta?Where are you from? (addressing a male)
مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتِ؟min ayna anti?Where are you from? (addressing a female)
أَنَا طَالِبٌanā ṭālibunI am a student (masculine).
أَنَا طَالِبَةٌanā ṭālibatunI am a student (feminine).

Pronunciation approximations are teaching aids, not substitutions for qualified audio models.

2. Worked dialogue

أ: مَرْحَبًا! مَا اسْمُكِ؟ — Hello! What is your name? (to a female)

ب: اِسْمِي لَيْلَى. — My name is Layla.

أ: مِنْ أَيْنَ أَنْتِ؟ — Where are you from? (to a female)

ب: أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَ. — I am from Egypt.

Evidence question: who shares a name and where they are from? Circle the question phrases and underline the answers.

Variation: replace the fictional name and origin with a different invented character. Preserve register or grammatical agreement where relevant.

3. Four-skill practice

  1. Listen: compare the questions and answers after a qualified language model. If none is available, mark listening as pending.
  2. Speak: practice the dialogue with a partner using fictional identities; switch roles and select an appropriate address form.
  3. Read: cover the meanings and identify the written questions, names and origin statements.
  4. Write: produce a three-line introduction of an invented character in the target script. Copy accurately before attempting independent recall.

Privacy rule: personal identity, legal name, address, or country of origin must never be required in a public learner exercise.

Foundation workshop — Introductions

Model: اِسْمِي نُور — my name is Noor.

Explicit teaching: اِسْمِي introduces an invented name; form of address depends on the person addressed.

Guided retrieval: Review one sound, letter or expression from the previous week; explain its meaning or form without using the answer column.

Independent transfer: Change the fictional name and ask for a name using an appropriate form.

Misconception check: Tell a partner which tempting interpretation would be incorrect and why. Instructors verify pronunciation using qualified speakers or reviewed audio rather than automatic speech scoring.

Teacher evidence: collect one short reading, one written form and a meaning explanation. Allow invented identities and accessible typed responses. This workshop supplements, rather than replaces, the existing lesson and quiz.

Four-skill evidence studio — Week 06

Original fictional reading:

أَنَا لَيْلَى. أَنَا مِنْ مِصْرَ. هَذَا صَدِيقِي سَامِي.

Reading task: Identify the speaker, country and the relationship mentioned. Complete your response before checking the meaning reference.

Teacher meaning reference (open after reading)

I am Layla. I am from Egypt. This is my friend Sami.

Listening task: With the text hidden, listen twice to a qualified instructor reading this passage or a separately approved recording. Record three details and a question. No approved audio is embedded here; this is an instructor-led task, not an automatically completed listening assessment.

Speaking task: Retell the main idea, then perform a brief original role-play on this week's topic. An instructor records independent speech, intelligibility, grammatical control and appropriateness; approximate transliteration is not scored as pronunciation.

Writing task: Write a fictional introduction of yourself or an invented character, using أَنَا and مِنْ. Revise after feedback and note one change.

Evidence rubric: Reading 0–3 (meaning supported by text); listening 0–3 (only with reviewed spoken source); speaking 0–3 (teacher-reviewed response); writing 0–3 (independent content, grammatical meaning and revision). Record each domain separately; incomplete domains remain unassessed. Weekly multiple-choice quiz is formative, not credit certification.

Private portfolio: Save course, week, student, date, original response, revision and instructor notes in the learner's private evidence collection; do not publish personal records to GitHub.

Continuing reading journey · Week 06

Original fictional practice text · Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), not a Saudi dialect recording:

مَرْحَبًا! أَنَا نُورٌ. مَا اسْمُكَ؟ اسْمِي سَامِي.

Read and notice: Reintroduce Noor and a second fictional speaker. مَا اسْمُكَ؟ addresses a male in this example; discuss the feminine form مَا اسْمُكِ with the teacher.

Independent evidence: Write an invented name exchange and label who is speaking.

Continuity journal: Preserve a private dated copy of the original response and one question for next week. This page does not automatically store or share student work.

Listening boundary: Do not score listening from text alone. Teacher-approved audio with an independently reviewed transcript is still required.

4. Week 06 formative quiz

Ten open-book questions. Eight correct responses meet the preliminary readiness threshold; separately assess listening, speaking and writing.

5. Teacher guidance

Pacing: five lessons of 30–45 minutes. Evidence: vocabulary recognition, paired dialogue, three-line introduction, and quiz. Accessibility: give large-print text, typing alternatives, non-speaking participation alternatives and unlimited formative retries.

Misconceptions: context changes formality; grammatical gender and role words do not justify assumptions about a learner. Teacher-verified pronunciations remain essential. No learner records are stored or transmitted by this page.

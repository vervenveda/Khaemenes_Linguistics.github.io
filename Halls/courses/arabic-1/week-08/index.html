Arabic I — Week 08

Asking and Answering Basic Questions

Goals: identify who/what/where questions, respond in simple statements, use polite conversation, and apply Weeks 01–07 knowledge.

1. Question formation

Modern Standard Arabic uses question words such as ما (what), مَنْ (who), and أَيْنَ (where). The particle هَلْ introduces many yes/no questions. Arabic also distinguishes grammatical forms of address; practice with fictional speakers and avoid assuming a student's identity.

Language exampleMeaning
مَا هَذَا؟What is this?
هَذَا كِتَابٌ.This is a book.
مَنْ هُوَ؟Who is he?
هُوَ طَالِبٌ.He is a student.
أَيْنَ لَيْلَى؟Where is Layla?
لَيْلَى هُنَا.Layla is here.
هَلْ هَذَا كِتَابٌ؟Is this a book?
نَعَمْ، هَذَا كِتَابٌ.Yes, this is a book.

The dialogue uses fictional participants. Check pronunciation with a qualified teacher or trusted recording. Do not rely on unverified text-to-speech for grading.

2. Read and rehearse

أ: مَرْحَبًا! مَا هَذَا؟ — Hello! What is this?

ب: هَذَا كِتَابٌ. — This is a book.

أ: هَلْ هَذَا كِتَابٌ جَدِيدٌ؟ — Is this a new book?

ب: نَعَمْ، هَذَا كِتَابٌ جَدِيدٌ. — Yes, this is a new book.

Comprehension: identify the first question, its answer, the yes/no question, and its answer. Underline the word or punctuation that signals a question.

3. Four-skill practice

  1. Listening: listen to a reviewed model of the dialogue twice; distinguish questions from statements.
  2. Speaking: practice with a partner, switch roles, and create one original question about a fictional object or person.
  3. Reading: match the eight written expressions above to English meanings without looking at the answer column.
  4. Writing: copy three questions and their answers, then construct a new question-and-answer pair. Request a teacher review of script and grammar.

4. Review Weeks 01–07

Use a retrieval checklist: writing direction and script identification; introductory letter patterns; pronunciation or stress awareness; greetings; introducing fictional names; personal pronouns; and simple sentences.

Unit performance: prepare a 4–6 line conversation between invented characters using greetings, an introduction, two questions, and appropriate answers. The teacher observes reading, oral communication and written evidence separately.

Foundation workshop — Questions

Model: أَيْنَ الْكِتَابُ؟ — where is the book?.

Explicit teaching: Question words request specific information; هَلْ is often used for yes/no questions.

Guided retrieval: Review one sound, letter or expression from the previous week; explain its meaning or form without using the answer column.

Independent transfer: Answer using an imagined classroom, and explain how a where-question differs from a yes/no question.

Misconception check: Tell a partner which tempting interpretation would be incorrect and why. Instructors verify pronunciation using qualified speakers or reviewed audio rather than automatic speech scoring.

Teacher evidence: collect one short reading, one written form and a meaning explanation. Allow invented identities and accessible typed responses. This workshop supplements, rather than replaces, the existing lesson and quiz.

Continuing reading journey · Week 08

Original fictional practice reading in Modern Standard Arabic; not certified Saudi dialect speech:

مَنْ أَنْتَ؟ أَنَا سَامِي. أَيْنَ الْبَيْتُ؟ هَذَا الْبَيْتُ.

Comprehension: Reuse the personal introductions and home vocabulary from Weeks 01–07. Distinguish مَنْ (who) from أَيْنَ (where), and compare the masculine أَنْتَ with feminine أَنْتِ.

Original writing: Compose three fictional lines with a who-question and a where-question. Keep work in a private portfolio, not in this public repository.

Audio boundary: No listening mastery without separately reviewed Arabic recordings and comprehension evidence.

5. Week 08 formative quiz

Ten open-book questions; score at least 8/10 for the preliminary readiness checkpoint. This is a weekly quiz and Unit 1 review, not the Week 16 midterm.

6. Teacher notes

Pacing: five sessions of 30–45 minutes. Evidence: comprehension, 4–6 line dialogue, vocabulary recall and weekly quiz. Accessibility: larger script text, untimed practice, supported writing, alternative oral/typed demonstration, and keyboard-friendly quiz.

Common issues: confusing who/what/where question words; treating intonation as the only clue; using English word order too literally; conflating language formality with student identity. Keep invented scenarios and student privacy intact.

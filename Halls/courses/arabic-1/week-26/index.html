Unit 04 · Week 26

Arabic I — Present tense patterns

Read, interpret, discuss and write expressions for everyday communication.

Vocabulary and language patterns

ExpressionMeaning
أَكْتُبُI write
تَكْتُبُyou (masculine singular) write / she writes (context)
يَكْتُبُhe writes
نَكْتُبُwe write
أَقْرَأُI read

Sample expressions are introductory: grammar, pronunciation and regional forms require qualified review in context.

Guided four-skill lesson

  1. Listening: identify the expressions when spoken by a qualified teacher or verified recording; otherwise mark this step pending.
  2. Speaking: practice a short fictional exchange and compare the expressions with earlier lessons.
  3. Reading: cover the English meanings and identify the expressions from the target script.
  4. Writing: copy five expressions and create a four-line fictional mini-dialogue using at least three accurately.

Independent application and reflection

Prepare a short scenario relevant to present tense patterns. Add a context note about language register or cultural differences, and describe one spelling or grammar challenge. Teacher reviews use of meaningful sentences rather than scoring vocabulary recognition alone.

Students may use fictional identities and circumstances; personal information must never be required.

Present-tense pattern workshop

Teacher model: Read the full target-language example, first for meaning and then for form. Ask students to notice what changed.

أَنَا أَكْتُبُ. — I write.

هُوَ يَكْتُبُ. — He writes.

نَحْنُ نَكْتُبُ. — We write.

هِيَ تَكْتُبُ. — She writes.

Explain: In this model verb, prefixes distinguish some present-tense persons; تَكْتُبُ can also mean 'you (masculine singular) write' when the addressee is clear. Forms depend on person, number and grammatical gender, and not all verbs follow the same pattern.

Guided transfer: Change هُوَ يَكْتُبُ to a sentence meaning 'We write.' State which part of the verb changes.

Misconception check: Ask the learner to explain the grammatical choice in their own words before independent writing. Do not mark pronunciation correct without qualified audio or teacher review.

Week 26 formative knowledge quiz

Ten open-book vocabulary recognition questions. The 80% threshold is provisional and does not replace speaking, listening and writing evidence.

Teacher notes and accessibility

Five adaptable sessions of 30–45 minutes: model, compare, rehearse, write, correct, reflect. Provide larger text, keyboard access, assistive writing, alternate speech assessment and ample retry time. No third-party libraries or remote record submission.

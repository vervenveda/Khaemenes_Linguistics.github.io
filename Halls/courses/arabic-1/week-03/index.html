Arabic I — Week 03

Connected Letterforms and Joining

Learning goals: identify key writing or sound patterns, practice accurate reading and writing, and explain the patterns with evidence.

1. Explanation and worked models

Arabic letters change shape depending on whether they stand alone or occur at the beginning, middle, or end of a connected sequence. Many letters connect on both sides; six letters (ا د ذ ر ز و) do not connect to the following letter. This is an introduction to shape recognition, not a full inventory of calligraphy rules.

ب　بـ　ـبـ　ـب

Bāʾ connects on both sides when its neighbors permit.

ت　تـ　ـتـ　ـت

Tāʾ follows the same joining pattern with two dots above.

ن　نـ　ـنـ　ـن

Nūn changes shape; distinguish its single dot above.

ا　ا　—　ـا

Alif can connect to a preceding letter but never to the following letter.

Connecting forms are determined by the letters on either side; an alif in the middle of a word can create a visible break in joining.

2. Four-skill guided lesson

  1. Listening: hear instructor-validated examples; mark unavailable recordings as pending rather than substituting unreviewed synthesized audio.
  2. Speaking: repeat and compare the target patterns, then explain your reasoning to a partner.
  3. Reading: match the model forms to their explanations and identify the crucial mark or shape.
  4. Writing: Trace isolated and initial/medial/final forms from right to left. Compare بَاب (bāb, door) and بِنْت (bint, girl): focus on joins rather than trying to master all vowel marks now.

Independent challenge: annotate two model examples and explain their differences without looking at the descriptions.

Teacher reminder: verify regional pronunciation and handwriting instructions. Avoid grading speech solely from text or browser speech synthesis.

Foundation workshop — Sound and script

Model: سَلَام — peace / greeting element.

Explicit teaching: Arabic consonant letters and optional vowel marks serve different functions. Pronunciation needs a reviewed spoken model.

Guided retrieval: Review one sound, letter or expression from the previous week; explain its meaning or form without using the answer column.

Independent transfer: Underline the written consonants in سَلَام and mark the vowel sign.

Misconception check: Tell a partner which tempting interpretation would be incorrect and why. Instructors verify pronunciation using qualified speakers or reviewed audio rather than automatic speech scoring.

Teacher evidence: collect one short reading, one written form and a meaning explanation. Allow invented identities and accessible typed responses. This workshop supplements, rather than replaces, the existing lesson and quiz.

3. Weekly knowledge quiz · 10 questions

Open-book formative quiz. Eight correct answers indicate a readiness checkpoint, not automatic mastery of pronunciation or handwriting.

4. Teacher notes and assessment evidence

Pacing: five adaptable sessions of 30–45 minutes. Evidence: annotated examples, handwriting practice, sound listening/speaking demonstration, quiz, and reflection. Accessibility: large text, readable examples, keyboard quiz, extra time, oral or assistive alternative to handwriting.

Instructor check: watch for over-generalizations from one spelling or example. These models are introductory and require teacher review before summative assessment.

Privacy: no network requests, no new dependencies, and no automatic learner record updates.

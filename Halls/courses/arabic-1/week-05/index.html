Arabic I — Week 05

Greetings and Introductions

Learning outcomes: greet politely, introduce oneself, ask another person's name, interpret a short dialogue, and copy key phrases accurately.

1. Core vocabulary and language choices

This lesson teaches introductory Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). Daily greetings vary across regions; explain MSA versus local spoken forms without marking dialects as incorrect.

Pronunciation guides are approximate. A qualified speaker or reviewed audio must support the listening and speaking activities.

2. Worked conversation

أ: مَرْحَبًا! مَا اسْمُكِ؟ (Hello! What is your name? (addressing a female))

ب: مَرْحَبًا! اسْمِي لَيْلَى. (Hello! My name is Layla.)

أ: تَشَرَّفْتُ بِمَعْرِفَتِكِ. (Pleased to meet you (addressing a female).)

ب: شُكْرًا. إِلَى اللِّقَاءِ! (Thank you. Goodbye!)

Comprehension: Who asks for a name? Who answers? Which expression shows politeness? Underline each in the dialogue.

Variation: Write another version of the dialogue replacing the name; preserve appropriate address and formality.

3. Practice all four skills

  1. Listening: listen twice to the dialogue with a qualified speaker; mark the activity pending if no accurate audio model is available.
  2. Speaking: act out the dialogue in pairs, then switch roles; use a respectful register.
  3. Reading: match four written greetings or names to their meanings, then cover the translations and reread the dialogue.
  4. Writing: write the greeting, your introduction, a question asking someone else's name, and a farewell in the target script. Use an invented example name if preferred.

Private learning: do not require students to publish their real name or other personal details to demonstrate the language skill.

4. Week 05 quiz

Ten formative open-book questions; 8/10 is the readiness guideline, with separate teacher assessment of oral interaction and written work.

5. Teacher notes and next steps

Pacing: five sessions of 30–45 minutes. Language targets: formulaic greetings, register, names, response and closing. Assessment evidence: a recorded or teacher-observed partner exchange (opt-in only), short written dialogue, and knowledge quiz. Accommodations: text enlargement, alternative oral or assisted responses, keyboard-accessible questions, and extra processing time.

Common misconceptions: not all greetings are interchangeable in every context; transliteration alone cannot capture the full pronunciation; names and forms of address deserve cultural respect. No account, paid service, or external dependency is required.

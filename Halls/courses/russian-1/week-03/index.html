Russian I — Week 03

Word Stress and Pronunciation

Learning goals: identify key writing or sound patterns, practice accurate reading and writing, and explain the patterns with evidence.

1. Explanation and worked models

Russian word stress is not fixed on a particular syllable. Stress can affect vowel pronunciation and sometimes changes word meaning or grammatical form. Stress marks are useful in teaching materials but are generally omitted in ordinary Russian writing.

ма́ма

MA-ma — The stress is on the first syllable.

вода́

va-DA — The unstressed о is commonly reduced in standard pronunciation.

молоко́

mə-la-KO — The final syllable is stressed; unstressed vowels often sound different.

за́мок / замо́к

ZÁ-mək / za-MÓK — A useful contrast: castle / lock; stress changes meaning.

Stress can distinguish meanings, as in за́мок (castle) versus замо́к (lock). Always verify stress in trustworthy teaching dictionaries.

2. Four-skill guided lesson

  1. Listening: hear instructor-validated examples; mark unavailable recordings as pending rather than substituting unreviewed synthesized audio.
  2. Speaking: repeat and compare the target patterns, then explain your reasoning to a partner.
  3. Reading: match the model forms to their explanations and identify the crucial mark or shape.
  4. Writing: Clap stressed syllables in ма́ма, вода́, and молоко́; circle the marked stressed vowel. Work with a qualified recording for vowel reduction. For handwriting, copy the Russian words, but do not treat the acute stress mark as obligatory in normal writing.

Independent challenge: annotate two model examples and explain their differences without looking at the descriptions.

Teacher reminder: verify regional pronunciation and handwriting instructions. Avoid grading speech solely from text or browser speech synthesis.

Foundation workshop — Stress and spelling

Model: вода́ — water (stress on the last syllable).

Explicit teaching: Stress marks are teaching aids normally omitted in everyday writing; stress affects vowel realization.

Guided retrieval: Recall one expression or script feature from a previous week and explain its meaning.

Independent transfer: Read вода́ with the indicated stress and explain why it matters.

Misconception check: Identify an incorrect interpretation or form and explain why it does not fit. Qualified teachers or recordings assess pronunciation.

Teacher evidence: collect reading, writing and meaning-based evidence without demanding private identities. This workshop supplements existing lesson and quiz.

3. Weekly knowledge quiz · 10 questions

Open-book formative quiz. Eight correct answers indicate a readiness checkpoint, not automatic mastery of pronunciation or handwriting.

4. Teacher notes and assessment evidence

Pacing: five adaptable sessions of 30–45 minutes. Evidence: annotated examples, handwriting practice, sound listening/speaking demonstration, quiz, and reflection. Accessibility: large text, readable examples, keyboard quiz, extra time, oral or assistive alternative to handwriting.

Instructor check: watch for over-generalizations from one spelling or example. These models are introductory and require teacher review before summative assessment.

Privacy: no network requests, no new dependencies, and no automatic learner record updates.

Russian I — Week 07

Personal Pronouns and Simple Statements

Learning goals: recognize six pronouns, read basic statements, and construct short present-time sentences respectfully.

1. Pronouns and grammar

Russian has personal pronouns and grammatical gender. In most basic present-tense statements, the copula 'am/is/are' is omitted. Pronunciation and stress still require checked sound models.

PronounApproximate pronunciationMeaning and context
яyaI
тыtyyou (informal singular)
выvyyou (polite singular or plural)
онonhe
онаonashe
мыmywe

Pronunciation guides are approximations; use teacher-validated audio for speech assessment. Grammatical gender and formality are language features, not assumptions about an individual learner.

2. Worked sentence models

  • Я студент. — I am a student (masculine noun form).
  • Я студентка. — I am a student (feminine noun form).
  • Он учитель. — He is a teacher.
  • Она учитель. — She is a teacher.

Notice: the pronoun and the noun phrase form a complete present-time statement without the English word “is” or “am.” Compare differences in grammatical form and meaning.

3. Four-skill practice

  1. Listening: hear each pronoun and statement from a qualified speaker; record the activity as pending if reliable audio is unavailable.
  2. Speaking: practice statements for invented characters, switching pronouns and ensuring the sentence remains grammatical.
  3. Reading: cover the English meanings and match each target-language statement to the correct interpretation.
  4. Writing: copy three model sentences, then create two new sentences about fictional characters. Ask a teacher to review grammatical agreement.

Student privacy: fictional characters are sufficient; never require the learner to disclose identity, home address, or other private information.

Foundation workshop — Pronouns

Model: Мы читаем. — we read.

Explicit teaching: Verb endings vary with the subject; many Russian present-time nominal sentences omit 'is'.

Guided retrieval: Recall one expression or script feature from a previous week and explain its meaning.

Independent transfer: Compare Я читаю and Мы читаем; explain what changed.

Misconception check: Identify an incorrect interpretation or form and explain why it does not fit. Qualified teachers or recordings assess pronunciation.

Teacher evidence: collect reading, writing and meaning-based evidence without demanding private identities. This workshop supplements existing lesson and quiz.

4. Week 07 formative quiz

Ten open-book questions; at least eight correct answers indicates preliminary readiness. Listening, speaking and writing are assessed separately.

5. Teacher notes

Pacing: five flexible sessions of approximately 30–45 minutes. Evidence: pronoun recognition, oral practice, short writing sample, quiz, and guided correction. Misconceptions: assuming English copula usage transfers word-for-word, confusing polite/informal forms or grammatical gender with personal identity.

Accessibility: untimed attempts, larger text, keyboard-accessible quiz, dictation support, typed writing alternatives, and optional non-speaking demonstrations. No external libraries or automatic student records.

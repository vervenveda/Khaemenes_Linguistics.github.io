Russian I — Week 01

Cyrillic letters and sounds

Objectives: orient to Cyrillic, recognize six starter letters, identify misleading letter shapes, and begin careful reading, listening, speaking, and handwriting practice.

1. Welcome to Cyrillic

Russian uses the Cyrillic alphabet, normally written from left to right. The modern Russian alphabet has 33 letters. Some look like familiar Latin letters and have similar sounds; others look familiar but represent different sounds.

Important: Russian uses variable word stress, and unstressed vowels may change their spoken quality. This week introduces printed letters, not a complete pronunciation or cursive system.

Speech models should come from a qualified speaker or carefully reviewed recording. Device speech tools are optional and must not be treated as a pronunciation examiner.

2. Six starter letters

А а
a

Vowel, as in stressed /a/. Unstressed vowels may sound different.

Б б
be

Consonant /b/.

В в
ve

Consonant /v/. Looks like Latin B, but sounds different.

Н н
en

Consonant /n/. Looks like Latin H.

Р р
er

Consonant /r/, often tapped or trilled. Looks like Latin P.

С с
es

Consonant /s/. Looks like Latin C.

Notice the potentially confusing look-alikes: В sounds like /v/, Н sounds like /n/, Р sounds like /r/, and С sounds like /s/. These are uppercase isolated print forms. Russian handwriting will be taught separately using verified models.

3. Practice all four language skills

  1. Listen: with a teacher or qualified recording, contrast /b/ (Б), /v/ (В), /n/ (Н), /r/ (Р), and /s/ (С). If audio is unavailable, note that listening evidence remains pending.
  2. Speak: pronounce each letter name and practice the consonant sounds; note that Russian /r/ differs from most common English /r/ pronunciations.
  3. Read: point to the printed letter the teacher names and contrast misleading Latin look-alikes.
  4. Write: copy uppercase and lowercase printed forms below. The displayed shapes are copy models; they do not teach full handwriting stroke order.

А а　Б б　В в　Н н　Р р　С с

Teacher checkpoint: confirm recognition of uppercase/lowercase pairs, support pen grip or assistive writing needs, and avoid penalizing novice accents on this first week.

4. Guided inquiry

  • Sort these into familiar-looking letters with different sounds versus those with similar sounds.
  • Explain to a partner why Н is not English H.
  • Write the six letters from memory, then check against the models.
  • Reflect on how a new script can change your assumptions about reading.

Cultural context: Russian is spoken across diverse communities and diaspora settings. This course studies language and culture without treating any government, ethnicity, or single region as representative of all speakers.

5. Week 01 quiz · 10 questions

Open-book formative assessment. An 8/10 score is the readiness guideline, alongside teacher observation of listening and writing. Try again as needed.

Teacher notes and assessment evidence

Pacing: five sessions, approximately 30–45 minutes each. Evidence: script identification check, handwriting sample, monitored sound distinctions, and a formative quiz.

Common misconceptions: confusing В/B, Н/H, Р/P, and С/C; assuming handwritten Cyrillic is the same as printed Cyrillic; thinking Russian stress is fixed; equating transliteration with pronunciation.

Accessibility: print-size magnification, keyboard-accessible quiz, untimed retakes, alternate writing tools and accommodations. Privacy: no network account, no third-party quiz service, no automatic student record.

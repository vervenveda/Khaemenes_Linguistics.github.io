Russian I — Week 02

Printed and Handwritten Cyrillic Forms

Objectives: recognize letter families and visual differences, explain writing orientation, and begin accurate copying with instructor-supported sound identification.

1. Understand the writing system

Russian print and cursive both use Cyrillic, but several handwritten forms differ substantially from their print equivalents. Learn to identify forms before trying fluent cursive.

This lesson uses reviewed descriptive guidance but not live speech recognition, AI grading, or invented pronunciations. Teachers should validate handwriting stroke order before demonstrating it.

2. Study the letter models

А а
a

Printed А/а: familiar-looking /a/ in stressed contexts.

Б б
be

Printed Б/б: /b/.

В в
ve

Printed В/в: /v/, not English B.

Н н
en

Printed Н/н: /n/, not English H.

Р р
er

Printed Р/р: /r/, not English P.

Т т
te

Printed Т/т: /t/. Russian cursive lowercase т often resembles a Latin m-like form.

Compare the forms

Cursive lowercase т is often m-shaped, while printed т resembles a Latin t in its lowercase form. Cursive lowercase п can look n-like, and cursive lowercase и can look u-like. Handwriting varies: compare verified models, not just a device's font.

3. Four-skill guided practice

  1. Listening: Listen to a reviewed recording of the letter names, then identify Б, В, Н, Р, Т aloud. Letter names and the phonemes they represent are related but not identical.
  2. Speaking: pronounce each reviewed sound after a teacher; explain which contrasts are unfamiliar.
  3. Reading: cover each caption, identify the character, and uncover to self-check.
  4. Writing: Begin with printed letter copies. Compare printed т, п, и against teacher-validated cursive models before attempting cursive. Avoid treating a generic italic computer font as a reliable stroke-order guide.
Copy models: А а　Б б　В в　Н н　Р р　Т т

Reflection: draw two easy-to-confuse forms, and explain their difference in your own words.

4. Independent learning check

Compare family patterns, check reading direction, and write a short paragraph explaining why a letter's appearance may not correspond to an English look-alike. Keep a handwritten copy for teacher review.

Foundation workshop — Sound-letter relationships

Model: ДОМ — house.

Explicit teaching: Russian letter-sound mappings depend partly on stress and neighboring sounds; teachers should provide reviewed audio.

Guided retrieval: Review one sound, letter or expression from the previous week; explain its meaning or form without using the answer column.

Independent transfer: Locate the vowel in ДОМ, then practice reading with a qualified model.

Misconception check: Tell a partner which tempting interpretation would be incorrect and why. Instructors verify pronunciation using qualified speakers or reviewed audio rather than automatic speech scoring.

Teacher evidence: collect one short reading, one written form and a meaning explanation. Allow invented identities and accessible typed responses. This workshop supplements, rather than replaces, the existing lesson and quiz.

5. Week 02 quiz

Ten open-book formative questions, with an 80% readiness guideline. A handwriting or oral check is still required; this quiz alone does not establish mastery.

Teacher notes

Pacing: five flexible sessions of approximately 30–45 minutes. Evidence: a copy sheet, oral identification or suitable accessibility alternative, quiz, and written reflection. Accommodations: enlarged models, tactile letters, assistive technology, untimed quizzes, speech alternatives, and teacher-reviewed writing supports.

Misconceptions: visual similarity does not guarantee the same sound; teacher-reviewed scripts are preferred over unverified automated pronunciation or stroke instructions. Respect regional language differences. No student information is sent or automatically stored.

# Khaemenes Linguistics — Year I Curriculum Deep-Dive and Upgrade Standard
Date: 2026-10-14
Courses: Arabic I and Russian I (Weeks 01–32)
Status: Instructional drafts; not externally validated or certified

## Audit findings
1. **Insufficient worked examples:** many Unit 02–04 lessons introduce short vocabulary lists but offer little explicit sentence-level demonstration, contrast, independent transfer or feedback on common learner misconceptions.
2. **Repetitive quizzes:** although the later multiple-choice prompts were diversified, questions remain mostly word recognition and generic study strategies. The quizzes cannot alone establish mastery of grammar or functional language.
3. **Unbalanced skills:** listening and speaking require qualified models not included in many public pages; writing tasks frequently lack sample answers and a task-specific analytic rubric.
4. **Arabic morphological scaffolding:** distinguish Modern Standard Arabic from dialect speech; explain gender, agreement, plural and verbal patterns progressively. Never infer students' personal identity from grammatical forms.
5. **Russian case and conjugation:** move from subject/nominative recognition to meaningful accusative examples and then controlled person/number verb changes. Address noun class and exceptions.
6. **Cultural and functional competence:** use realistic, respectful settings with fictional people and choices; avoid reducing culture to flags or stereotypes. Compare speech registers and dialects responsibly.
7. **Course validity:** midterm/final pages are blueprints, not secure unseen tests. Qualified subject review, instructor audio, robust rubrics and full browser validation are release gates.

## Required standard for every weekly lesson
A. **Measurable goals:** 2–3 outcomes spanning form, meaning and communicative use.
B. **Retrieval warm-up:** ask two questions from prior weeks with one model answer.
C. **Vocabulary in context:** 5–8 terms plus 3 complete authentic-sounding sample sentences or a short cohesive dialogue; translate and annotate.
D. **Explicit teaching:** explanation of a specific grammar/conversation feature and a correct/incorrect contrast.
E. **Guided practice:** one supported transformation or question/answer task.
F. **Four-skill practice:** reading evidence; handwriting/typing with feedback; oral model reviewed by teacher; listening that may be marked pending if verified material is unavailable.
G. **Independent task:** create a short message, dialogue or description using fictional names/places.
H. **Misconception check:** explain why a plausible wrong form is inappropriate in context.
I. **Formative assessment:** ten unique prompts with at least three meaning-in-context/grammar items plus a teacher-scored written response; 80% on multiple choice is not itself mastery.
J. **Teacher resources:** answer rationale, language variation notes, accessibility paths, and student privacy safeguards.

## Four new worked-language model demonstrations

- Arabic Week 26: present-tense person prefixes with clear contextual ambiguity.
- Russian Week 18: nominative/accusative sentence contrasts for feminine -а nouns.
- Russian Week 26: first conjugation person distinctions with teacher guidance.

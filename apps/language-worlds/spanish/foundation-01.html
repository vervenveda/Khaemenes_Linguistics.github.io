Spanish · Foundation Lesson 01

Introducing yourself and respectful greetings

Learning goal: Use greetings, a personal introduction, and a question naturally.

Supplementary guided lesson. This practice does not replace the Hall's formal curriculum or establish course credit.

Read and understand

Hola. Me llamo Ana. ¿Cómo te llamas? — Me llamo Luis. Mucho gusto. — Igualmente.

Hello. My name is Ana. What is your name? — My name is Luis. Nice to meet you. — Likewise.

Speech synthesis is optional. The written passage remains available offline.

Study the language

  1. Me llamo means 'my name is'; ¿Cómo te llamas? asks someone their name.
  2. Buenos días is commonly used in the morning; buenas tardes later in the day. Greetings vary across communities.
  3. Spanish question marks appear at both the beginning and end: ¿...?
  4. Pronounce the vowels a, e, i, o, u clearly and avoid treating Spanish spelling like English.

Repeat the passage, then write a new, fictional introduction and question. Do not use personal identifying information for public assignments.

Five-question challenge

Complete all five questions. Four correct responses (80%) indicate a knowledge checkpoint only; authentic reading, writing, listening and speaking still require review.

1. Which phrase asks someone's name?
2. Which greeting is generally appropriate in the morning?
3. What does 'Me llamo Ana' mean?
4. What punctuation opens a written Spanish question?
5. Which phrase responds politely to an introduction?

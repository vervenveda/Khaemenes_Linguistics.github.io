Japanese · Foundation Lesson 01

Greetings, self-introduction and writing systems

Learning goal: Introduce yourself politely and identify hiragana, katakana, and kanji.

Supplementary guided lesson. This practice does not replace the Hall's formal curriculum or establish course credit.

Read and understand

こんにちは。わたしは はな です。おなまえは なんですか。— けん です。よろしくおねがいします。

Hello. I am Hana. What is your name? — I'm Ken. Pleased to meet you / I look forward to our acquaintance.

Speech synthesis is optional. The written passage remains available offline.

Study the language

  1. こんにちは (konnichiwa) is a common daytime greeting.
  2. わたしは…です introduces oneself in a simple polite sentence; the topic particle は is pronounced 'wa' here.
  3. Hiragana, katakana and kanji are distinct elements of Japanese writing; each serves important functions.
  4. よろしくおねがいします is context-sensitive; no single English expression captures every use.

Repeat the passage, then write a new, fictional introduction and question. Do not use personal identifying information for public assignments.

Five-question challenge

Complete all five questions. Four correct responses (80%) indicate a knowledge checkpoint only; authentic reading, writing, listening and speaking still require review.

1. Which is a common daytime greeting?
2. Which script commonly writes many grammatical endings?
3. Which script often represents foreign loanwords?
4. In わたしは, how is topic particle は pronounced?
5. What is the role of です in a simple introduction?
